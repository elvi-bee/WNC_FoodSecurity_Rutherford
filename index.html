The Geography of Going Hungry: Food Insecurity Across Western North Carolina

Western North Carolina is a region of striking contrasts. Its mountains, forests, and rivers draw visitors from across the country, yet beneath that natural beauty and abundance lies a persistent and uneven struggle — about one in four people across the region cannot count on their next meal.

Food insecurity is the condition of not having reliable access to enough affordable, nutritious food. The eighteen counties of the Western North Carolina Health Network reported food insecurity rates ranging from 16.6% to 41.7% in 2024, with a regional average of 27.4% — well above national benchmarks.

Rutherford County: A Case Study in Rural Food Insecurity

With a food insecurity rate of 41.7% — more than 14 percentage points above the regional average — Rutherford County faces one of the most significant food access challenges in Western North Carolina. Nearly half of all residents experience some form of food insecurity, and the county’s SNAP household count is more than double the regional average, reflecting the depth of need.

Population and Poverty by Census Tract

County-level statistics tell only part of the story. Zooming in to the census tract level reveals where within Rutherford County need is most concentrated — and where it may be most visible to service providers, or most hidden from them. The maps below show population density and poverty rate side by side across Rutherford’s census tracts. Darker colors indicate higher values. Areas of high poverty do not always coincide with areas of high population density — a pattern that has real consequences for where food assistance resources are located and who can realistically reach them.

Food Pantry Access: Who Can Get There?

The map below shows the locations of food pantries across Rutherford County alongside drive time isochrones, the areas reachable within 10 and 20 minutes by car from any pantry. The areas that fall outside both rings represent communities where pantry access requires a significant investment of time and transportation. The gaps on this map are not empty space. They are where the need is hardest to meet.
